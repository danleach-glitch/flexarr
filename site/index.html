Included with the server

Up and running in minutes.

Flexarr runs on a computer at home that stays on: a NAS, a home server, a Mac mini or a desktop PC. It's free, and your films and music stay where they are.

Install the server

Choose what it will run on.

With Docker installed, one command starts Flexarr. Change /path/to/media to the folder that holds your films, shows and music.

docker run -d --name flexarr --restart unless-stopped \
  -p 8383:8383 \
  -v flexarr-config:/config \
  -v /path/to/media:/data/media \
  ghcr.io/danleach-glitch/flexarr:latest

Prefer Compose, or want hardware video conversion? See the Docker guide.

On DSM 7.2 or later, Flexarr runs in Container Manager as a project: make two folders, paste a short Compose file and start it.

Step by step for Synology

Add a container on the Docker tab with the repository ghcr.io/danleach-glitch/flexarr:latest, port 8383, an appdata folder for /config and your media share.

Step by step for Unraid

On a Mac (Apple silicon or Intel), Flexarr runs natively as a background service, using the Mac's own hardware to convert video. It's built from the source with one command.

Step by step for macOS

On Windows, Flexarr runs in Docker Desktop. Install it, then run the same command as on Linux in PowerShell, with your media folder in place of /path/to/media.

Step by step for Windows

Open it in your browser

On a computer at home, go to http://<your-server>:8383 and choose Set up Flexarr. The first browser to reach a new server becomes its owner: no account, no key. Then add your film, TV and music folders in Settings › Libraries.

First setup, in detail

Connect your Apple TV and iPhone

Open the app on the same Wi-Fi. It looks for your server, or you can type in its address. Then it shows a short code: enter it on your computer under Settings › Access › Connect a device, and that device is signed in for good.

Connecting devices, and what to do if one can't see your server

Add your *arrs Recommended

In Settings › Connections, add Sonarr, Radarr and Lidarr (and Seerr, for requests) for artwork, details and new downloads in seconds. Mount your media at the same path they use, so their paths match what Flexarr sees.

Good to know.

Is it really free?

Yes, for personal and other noncommercial use, under the PolyForm Noncommercial License. There's no account, no subscription and nothing to unlock. The source is on GitHub.

Do I need Sonarr, Radarr and Lidarr?

They're recommended, not required. Flexarr takes artwork and details for films and shows from Radarr and Sonarr, so without them your files still play but look plain. Music works well without Lidarr, from your files' own tags and cover art. More on what you need.

What do I need to run it?

A computer at home that stays on and can see your media: a NAS, a Linux or Windows machine with Docker, or a Mac. The Docker image runs on Intel, AMD and ARM 64-bit processors. A Mac can also use its own hardware to convert video quickly.

Does anything leave my home?

Your files and what you watch stay on your server. A few things are sent only if you use them: notifications to your phone pass through flexarr.io on their way to Apple, and error reports are off unless you turn them on. Read the privacy policy.

Can I watch away from home?

Yes. Tailscale connects your phone to your server from anywhere without opening ports on your router, and the apps switch over by themselves. How to set it up.

When is the Apple TV app on the App Store?

Soon. Until then you can install it on your own Apple TV from a Mac with Xcode. Here's how. There isn't an Android app yet, but the web app works in any browser.
