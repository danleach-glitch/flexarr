    Frequently asked questions

    Short answers about Flexarr's cost and licence, what you need to run it, privacy, the apps, and how many people can use it.

    #The basics

    #What is Flexarr?

    Flexarr is a media server you run yourself, on a computer at home. It organises your films, TV, music and podcasts and plays them on the web, Apple TV, iPhone and iPad. There's no Flexarr account and no cloud service in the middle: your library and everything about it stays on your server.

    It's built to sit alongside Sonarr, Radarr and Lidarr, which fetch and organise your media, and Seerr, which handles requests.

    #Is it free?

    Yes. The server and the iPhone and iPad app are both free, with no subscription and no adverts.

    Because it restricts commercial use, Flexarr is source-available rather than open source in the Open Source Initiative's sense. The source code is all on GitHub.

    If you'd like to support it, there's a Sponsor button on the GitHub page.

    #Do I need Sonarr, Radarr or Lidarr?

    Flexarr works best with them, and they're where most of its information comes from. Here's what each one adds:

    • Sonarr and Radarr supply the posters, artwork and details for your shows and films, with no extra accounts or API keys. Without them, Flexarr still finds and plays your video files, but titles show without artwork or descriptions. They also power Coming soon, deleting through Sonarr and Radarr, Fix match, and "find a better copy" in Library health.
    • Lidarr supplies artist pictures, album covers and the split into albums, EPs and singles. Music works without it, using the tags in your files and any artwork in their folders.
    • Seerr lets you request films and shows from Discover. Without it, Discover shows your own library by genre.

    Cast and crew for TV shows come from TVmaze, which needs no setup. Podcasts are built in and need none of these apps. See Connect Sonarr, Radarr, Lidarr and Seerr.

    #What hardware do I need?

    Any computer that stays on at home and can see your media:

    • A Mac, Apple silicon or Intel. Flexarr runs natively, and uses the Mac's graphics chip to convert video when a device needs it. See Install on a Mac.
    • A NAS or Linux machine with Docker. The image runs on Intel/AMD (x86-64) and ARM (arm64) machines. See Synology, Unraid and Docker.

    Most of the time the server does very little: the Apple TV, iPhone and iPad play files as they are, and the server sends the bytes. It works harder when it has to convert a video for a device that can't play it, such as a web browser with a 4K HEVC film. A graphics chip that can do this (Apple's VideoToolbox, Intel Quick Sync, VA-API or NVIDIA NVENC) makes a big difference.

    The apps need iOS, iPadOS or tvOS 18.2 or later. More in What you need.

    #Using it

    #Can several people use it?

    Yes. Each person gets a profile with their own Continue Watching, watched history, playlists, podcasts and settings, which follow them from device to device. Profiles can have a PIN, and can be kept out of particular libraries. A shared Apple TV can ask who's watching each time Flexarr opens.

    The main profile manages the server; the others can change their own playback and podcast settings. See Profiles.

    #How many people can watch at once?

    Flexarr doesn't limit how many screens play at once. In practice the limit is your server and your network.

    • Files played as they are (most playback on Apple TV, iPhone and iPad) take very little work, so many can run at once.
    • Converted streams take real work. Flexarr converts up to 3 videos at once by default, which suits a Mac mini. When that's full, the next person to start one is told the server is busy, and one profile can't take every slot. You can change the number with maxConcurrentTranscodes under transcoding in config.json (0 means no limit), then restart Flexarr.
    • Live TV is limited by how many connections your IPTV provider allows, though everyone watching the same channel shares one.

    #Does it work away from home?

    Yes, through Tailscale, a free service that links your own devices privately. Nothing is opened on your router, and Flexarr isn't put on the public internet. Once remote access is on, the apps switch to it by themselves when you leave home, and back again when you return. You can share it by inviting someone to your tailnet and giving them a profile.

    If you'd rather use your own HTTPS reverse proxy, that works too. See Away from home.

    #Can I watch offline?

    On iPhone and iPad, yes: download films, episodes, albums, playlists and podcast episodes. What you watch offline counts once you're back online. See Downloads.

    #Privacy

    #Does any data leave my home?

    Your library, what everyone watches, your profiles and your logs stay on your server. Flexarr has no account, analytics or adverts. A few things do go out, each for a reason:

    • Artwork and details. The server downloads posters and artwork from the image addresses Sonarr, Radarr and Lidarr give it, and TV cast from TVmaze.
    • Podcasts. Browsing and search use Apple's podcast directory, and the server fetches each show's feed and episodes. Your devices play them through your server, so podcast hosts see your server rather than each listener.
    • Live TV logos, if you use Live TV, come from a community collection on GitHub.
    • Notifications, if you turn them on, pass through a small relay at flexarr.io on their way to Apple. They're encrypted on your server so only your phone can read them; the relay never sees what's in them and keeps nothing.
    • Error reports, only if you turn them on. They describe what went wrong in Flexarr's code, with titles, names, addresses and keys removed first. See Logs and diagnostics.
    • Plex profile pictures, only if you use that button, make one request to plex.tv. See Bring your history over from Plex.

    The apps talk only to your server. The full details are in the privacy policy.

    #Apps

    #Is there an Apple TV app on the App Store?

    Not yet. The iPhone and iPad app is on the App Store. The Apple TV app is coming to the App Store next; until then, you can install it on your own Apple TV from a Mac with Xcode. See Apple TV with Xcode.

    #Is there an Android app?

    Not at the moment. Android, Android TV and Chromecast are on the roadmap under Later, with no date yet. In the meantime, the web app works in any modern browser, including on Android phones and tablets.

    #Does it work with Siri?

    On iPhone and iPad, yes: "Play the next episode of Curb Your Enthusiasm in Flexarr", "Shuffle Family Guy in Flexarr", and podcast commands too. See Siri and Shortcuts.

    #Getting help

    #Where do I report a bug?

    On GitHub: github.com/danleach-glitch/flexarr/issues. Say what happened and what you expected, and attach the zip from Download diagnostics in Settings › Logs. See Troubleshooting.

    #Where do I ask for a feature?

    In Ideas on GitHub Discussions. The roadmap links each planned feature to its discussion, so you can add your vote and thoughts there.

    #What's changed in each version?

    Settings › About in the web app shows the latest release, and the full changelog is on GitHub. To upgrade, see Updating.
